# MAEG1020 Computational Design and Fabrication

**Phase 2 | Priority: ★★★**  
**Why critical**: CAD, generative methods and digital fabrication links between design intent and manufacturable geometry.

---

## 1. 5 Core Mental Models Every Expert Shares

1. **Geometry is the Interface Between Design and Manufacture** — What you can represent, you can more easily make.
2. **Parametric and Generative Models Encode Intent** — Changing parameters should update the whole design consistently.
3. **Tolerance and Manufacturing Constraints Belong in the Model** — Late discovery of unmakeable features is expensive.
4. **Simulation is Only as Good as the Assumptions** — FEA and kinematic simulation can mislead if boundary conditions are wrong.
5. **Digital Thread Links Design, Analysis and Fabrication** — Traceability from concept to part reduces errors.

---

## 2. 3 Places Experts Fundamentally Disagree

### Disagreement 1: CAD Mastery vs First-Principles Sketching First
- CAD enables complexity and iteration. Hand sketching builds spatial judgment and prevents tool-driven design.

### Disagreement 2: How Early to Introduce Generative / Topology Optimization
- Early: exposes students to modern practice. Later: classical design rules should be solid first.

### Disagreement 3: Design for Additive vs Design for Traditional Processes
- Additive frees geometry. Traditional processes still dominate volume production and many structural parts.

## 3. 10 Questions that Distinguish Deep Understanding

1. What information is lost when you convert a parametric model to a mesh for 3D printing?
2. Why can a design that looks fine in CAD be impossible or expensive to machine?
3. How do you decide the level of detail that belongs in an early conceptual CAD model?
4. What is the difference between a kinematic simulation and a full dynamic simulation of a robot?
5. When is topology optimization likely to produce a better part than classical sizing?
6. How would you embed assembly constraints so that a multi-body robot model updates correctly?
7. What checks should you run before sending a soft-gripper mold design to a 3D printer?
8. Why do digital twins require more than just a CAD model?
9. How does the choice of CAD kernel affect interoperability with analysis and CAM tools?
10. In your hybrid arm, which parts are best designed parametrically and which can stay simple solids?
