## 1. 5 Core Mental Models Every Expert Shares

1. **Stimulus → Response is the Defining Feature** — Smart materials change properties or shape under electric, magnetic, thermal or chemical fields.
2. **Energy Conversion Efficiency and Bandwidth Trade Off** — High force density often comes with slow response or low efficiency (classic SMA example).
3. **Hysteresis and Nonlinearity are the Norm** — Most smart materials are strongly hysteretic; control must account for it.
4. **Integration Beats Raw Material Performance** — The system (electrodes, packaging, thermal path, control) usually limits practical performance more than the material itself.
5. **Multi-Functionality is the Opportunity** — Sensing and actuation can be combined in the same material element.

---

## 2. 3 Places Experts Fundamentally Disagree

### Disagreement 1: SMA vs Pneumatic vs EAP as Primary Soft Actuation
- **SMA**: high force, silent, simple. **Pneumatic**: mature, fast, large stroke. **EAP/DEA**: potential for muscle-like performance but still immature for many loads.

### Disagreement 2: Material Discovery vs System Integration as the Bottleneck
- **New materials** are exciting. Many argue that packaging, control and reliability of existing materials are the real barriers to adoption.

### Disagreement 3: Biologically Inspired vs Engineering-First Design
- **Bio-inspiration** yields novel mechanisms. **Engineering-first** optimizes for the actual constraints of the application.

## 3. 10 Questions that Distinguish Deep Understanding

1. Why do shape-memory alloys exhibit large hysteresis, and how does that affect closed-loop control?
2. What limits the cycle life of a typical SMA actuator?
3. Compare the work density of SMA, pneumatic actuators and human muscle — what does the comparison miss?
4. How does the glass-transition or melting behaviour of a polymer influence an electroactive polymer actuator?
5. Why is dielectric elastomer actuation often limited by pull-in instability or dielectric breakdown?
6. In what applications would you prefer a piezoelectric actuator over an electromagnetic one?
7. How would you model the thermo-mechanical behaviour of an SMA wire for a soft-gripper design?
8. What are the main practical obstacles to using ionic polymer-metal composites (IPMC) outside the laboratory?
9. How can a smart material element be used simultaneously as sensor and actuator?
10. For your hybrid arm, where could smart materials replace or augment the current pneumatic soft gripper?
