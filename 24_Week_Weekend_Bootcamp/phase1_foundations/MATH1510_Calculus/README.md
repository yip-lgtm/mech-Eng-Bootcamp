# MATH1510 Calculus for Engineers

**Phase 1 | Priority: ★★★**  
**Why critical**: Core mathematical language for rates, accumulation and modelling in every engineering course.

---

## 1. 5 Core Mental Models Every Expert Shares

1. **Derivative as Instantaneous Rate** — Not just the slope of a tangent line.
2. **Integral as Accumulated Quantity** — Net change, area, work, charge, etc.
3. **Fundamental Theorem Links the Two** — Differentiation and integration are inverse processes.
4. **Approximation is Central** — Linearization, Taylor series and numerical methods all start from limits.
5. **Modelling Before Solving** — Translate a physical situation into mathematics first.

---

## 2. 3 Places Experts Fundamentally Disagree

### Disagreement 1: Limits Rigour vs Intuitive Approach
- Rigour prevents later confusion. Intuition first keeps engineering students engaged.

### Disagreement 2: Symbolic Mastery vs Numerical Methods Emphasis
- Symbolic builds exact understanding. Numerical is what is used in practice.

### Disagreement 3: How Much Proof in an Engineering Calculus Course
- Some proof develops reasoning. Heavy proof displaces useful techniques.

## 3. 10 Questions that Distinguish Deep Understanding

1. Why does existence of a derivative imply continuity, but not vice versa?
2. Difference between average and instantaneous rate of change?
3. How does the Fundamental Theorem justify antiderivatives for definite integrals?
4. When is linear approximation good enough, and how do you quantify error?
5. Why can two different functions have the same derivative?
6. Geometric meaning of the definite integral of velocity?
7. What does series convergence mean, and why does absolute convergence matter?
8. How do you decide substitution, parts or partial fractions?
9. Why is the chain rule necessary for compositions?
10. In a physical context, what does a negative definite integral of a positive integrand usually indicate?
