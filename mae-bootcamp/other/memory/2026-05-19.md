# 📘 5MM — 5 Mental Models (五個心智模型)

## MM-1: Load Path Continuity (荷載路徑連續性)

**Definition:** A structural system is only as strong as its ability to transfer loads from the point of application, through the structural members, down to the foundation, and ultimately into the ground — without any "weak link" in the chain.

**Equation (RSE format, per Buildings Department Hong Kong):**

$$w_{u} = \gamma_g G_k + \gamma_q Q_k \quad \text{(Eurocode 0, BS EN 1990:2002+A1:2005)}$$

Where:
- $w_u$ = ultimate limit state design load (kN/m²)
- $\gamma_g = 1.35$ (dead load partial factor)
- $\gamma_q = 1.50$ (imposed load partial factor)
- $G_k$ = characteristic dead load
- $Q_k$ = characteristic imposed (live) load

**Concrete example from 2026-05-19 work:**

For the Internal Staircase report (MW 1.1):
- Span $L = 3.0$ m, Width $b = 1.2$ m, Thickness $t = 150$ mm
- Design load $w_u = 11.45$ kN/m² at ULS
- Verification: $M_{Ed} = w_u L^2 / 8 = 11.45 \times 3.0^2 / 8 = 12.88$ kN·m/m
- $V_{Ed} = w_u L / 2 = 11.45 \times 1.5 = 17.18$ kN/m

The "load path" mental model requires checking:
1. Slab → Stair stringer → Landing beam → Column → Foundation
2. **Every node** in this chain must have capacity $\geq$ demand

**Citation:** Eurocode 2 (BS EN 1992-1-1:2004+A1:2014); Buildings Department (Hong Kong) PNAP APP-21 (2017 revision); MacGregor 1997.

**為何呢個模型咁重要 / Why this model matters:**
如果工程師只計算 individual members 但忽略 load path continuity，成個 system 可能 collapse，即使每個 member 個別都 "adequate"。1995 Sampoong Department Store collapse (Korea, 502 deaths) 就係典型嘅 load path failure — 冷氣機改動切斷咗 critical column 嘅 load path。

## MM-2: Robustness & Progressive Collapse Resistance (穩健性)

**Definition (per U.K. Building Regulations Approved Document A, 2013 edition):**

> "The structure should be designed to sustain a limited extent of accidental damage without disproportionate collapse."

**Equation / 概念框架 (three-tier per Eurocode 1-1-7:2006):**

| Class | Building Type | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low consequence (≤ 4 storeys) | No specific measures |
| 2a | Medium (4 storeys ≤ h ≤ 15 storeys) | Horizontal ties + vertical ties |
| 2b | Medium (≥ 15 storeys OR hospital) | Horizontal + vertical ties + check for loss of column |
| 3 | High consequence (>15 storeys, post-disaster function) | Risk assessment + alternative load path analysis |

**Citation:** U.K. BRE Digest 436 (2010 revision); Starossek 2009 (Hamburg) "Progressive Collapse of Structures"; NIST report on Ronan Point (1972, 1975).

**Historical anchor:** Ronan Point (1968 London gas explosion) triggered entire robustness framework. 2026-05-19 嘅 Protective Barrier report (MW 1.6) 屬於 Class 1，但 supporting structure (MW 1.50) 同 underground drain (MW 1.36) 喺 robustness tie requirement 上有唔同 trigger。

**中英對照 / Bilingual note:**
- EN: "Robustness" = ability to withstand local failure without disproportionate spread
- 中: 結構穩健性 = 局部失效唔會引致成體系崩塌嘅能力
- 兩者都強調 "disproportionate"，即 failure extent 遠超 original cause。

## MM-3: Limit State Design Philosophy (極限狀態設計)

**Definition:** Design every structural member for TWO limit states:
- **ULS (Ultimate Limit State):** safety against collapse — load factor 1.35/1.50
- **SLS (Serviceability Limit State):** functionality — deflection ≤ L/250, crack width ≤ 0.3 mm

**Equation:**

$$E_d \leq R_d \quad \text{(Eurocode 0, basis of design)}$$

Where $E_d$ = effect of design actions, $R_d$ = design resistance.

**Numerical example from 2026-05-19:**

Internal staircase check (MW 1.1):
- ULS moment demand: $M_{Ed} = 12.88$ kN·m/m
- ULS moment capacity (assume 12 mm @ 150 c/c, C30): $M_{Rd} = \min(0.167 f_{ck} b d^2, f_{yd} A_s z)$
- For C30, d = 125 mm: $M_{Rd} = 0.167 \times 30 \times 1000 \times 125^2 / 10^6 = 78.3$ kN·m/m ✓ Adequate
- SLS check: deflection = $5 w_k L^4 / (384 E I) = ?$ — typically L/250 = 12 mm, must check

**Citation:** Beeby & Narayanan 1995 (concrete designer's companion); Mosley & Bungey 2012 (limit state philosophy); Eurocode 2 introduction §5.

**Industrial implication:** 識用 limit state 嘅工程師識得分 "safety margin" 同 "functionality" 嘅 distinction；唔識嘅工程師會混淆 "is it safe" 同 "is it fit for purpose"。

## MM-4: Kinematic Chain in Robotics (運動鏈)

**Definition (per Craig 2005, Spong et al. 2006):** A robot manipulator is a series of rigid bodies (links) connected by joints, forming an open or closed kinematic chain. The relationship between joint coordinates $\mathbf{q}$ and end-effector pose $\mathbf{x}$ is given by forward kinematics.

**Equation — Forward Kinematics (DH convention):**

$$^{i-1}T_i = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_i & -\sin\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & \sin\theta_i\sin\alpha_i & a_i\cos\theta_i \\ \sin\theta_i & \cos\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & -\cos\theta_i\sin\alpha_i & a_i\sin\theta_i \\ 0 & \sin\alpha_i & \cos\alpha_i & d_i \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Equation — Inverse Kinematics (2-link planar example):**

$$\theta_2 = \arccos\left(\frac{x^2 + y^2 - l_1^2 - l_2^2}{2 l_1 l_2}\right)$$

$$\theta_1 = \arctan2(y, x) - \arctan2(l_2 \sin\theta_2, l_1 + l_2 \cos\theta_2)$$

**Citation:** Craig 2005 *Introduction to Robotics*; Spong, Hutchinson & Vidyasagar 2006 *Robot Modeling and Control*; Siciliano & Khatib 2016 *Springer Handbook of Robotics*; Paul 1981 *Robot Manipulators* (DH convention).

**Cross-domain link to MM-1:** A structural member 同 robot link 都係 rigid body。Structural load path = robot joint force/torque transmission. 兩者都遵循 **Newton-Euler equations**。

**2026-05-19 relevance:** Week 2 Robotics Mechanisms 開展，學習 DH parameters 同 Jacobian。Gary demo 展示咗 ROS integration。

## MM-5: Probabilistic Basis vs Deterministic Code (概率 vs 確定性)

**Definition:** Structural codes 表面睇係 deterministic，但背後有一個隱藏嘅 **target reliability index** $\beta$ (per Eurocode 0, Annex C):

$$\beta = \frac{\mu_R - \mu_S}{\sqrt{\sigma_R^2 + \sigma_S^2}}$$

Typical target: $\beta = 3.8$ for 50-year reference period (failure probability ≈ $10^{-4}$/year).

**Equation — reliability transformation (FORM, per Hasofer-Lind 1974):**

$$P_f \approx \Phi(-\beta) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{-\beta} e^{-t^2/2} dt$$

**Citation:** Hasofer & Lind 1974 (exact invariance); Melchers & Beck 2018 *Structural Reliability Analysis*; Eurocode 0 (BS EN 1990) Annex C; JCSS Probabilistic Model Code 2001.

**Philosophical tension:** RSE 報告出嘅 "Adequate ✅" 唔係 absolute truth，而係 $P_f < 10^{-4}$ 嘅 conditional statement on assumed load model、material model 同 workmanship。

**中英對照 / Bilingual insight:**
- 確定性設計 = one-point estimate (e.g., $w = 11.45$ kN/m²)
- 概率設計 = distribution + integration (e.g., $w \sim \text{Gumbel}, \mu_w = 8, \sigma_w = 1.5$)
- 後者更真實，前者更易用。Eurocode 用 semi-probabilistic (partial factor) approach 係 compromise。

# ⚔️ 3DG — Three Fundamental Disagreements (三個根本分歧)

## DG-1: Allowable Stress Design (ASD) vs Limit State Design (LSD)

| Position | Position A: ASD (American tradition) | Position B: LSD (European/UK tradition) |
|---|---|---|
| **核心思想** | Single safety factor on stress (e.g., $f_{allow} = f_y / FS$, $FS = 1.67$) | Separate factors on load AND resistance (e.g., $\gamma_g = 1.35, \gamma_q = 1.50, \gamma_c = 1.50$) |
| **Safety philosophy** | One global "factor of ignorance" | Different uncertainties treated differently (dead load less variable than live load) |
| **Serviceability** | Often implicit | Explicit SLS check (deflection, crack, vibration) |
| **Implementation** | ACI 318 Appendix A (historical); AISC pre-1986 | ACI 318 Chapter 9 (current "strength design" = LSD); Eurocode 2; BS 8110 (withdrawn 2010) |
| **Citation** | Whitney 1937, ACI 318-63 | MacGregor 1997; Beeby & Narayanan 1995 |

**The tension:** ASD is conceptually simpler (one number) and easier to teach. LSD is conceptually richer but introduces partial factors that look arbitrary to newcomers. Real disagreement is **philosophical**: do we treat uncertainty as "single ignorance" or as "multiple distinct ignorance"?

**Historical anchor:** ACI 318 first adopted LSD ("strength design") in 1963, then gradually transitioned; by 1999 all ACI codes were LSD-only. Eurocode adoption in Hong Kong accelerated after 2004.

**2026-05-19 application:** MW 1.1 report uses LSD (Eurocode basis) with $w_u = 11.45$ kN/m² ULS — explicit $\gamma$ factors in calculation sheet.

## DG-2: Redundancy vs Efficiency (冗餘 vs 效率)

| Position | Position A: Highly Redundant | Position B: Efficient & Lean |
|---|---|---|
| **核心思想** | Add alternate load paths (more members, more ties, more capacity than strictly needed) | Minimize material; trust calculation; "just enough" capacity |
| **Failure mode tolerated** | Loss of any single member | Only design loads (no accidental scenario) |
| **Cost** | +15-25% material | Lower cost, lower embodied carbon |
| **Citation** | Brett & Lu 2013 (robustness in NZ); NIST 2007 (post-9/11 recommendations) | CIB 2008 (sustainability); Allwood 2012 (material efficiency) |

**The tension:** Lean design saves material (lower embodied carbon, lower cost) but may fail disproportionately under accidental loads. Redundant design is safer but wasteful. The disagreement is essentially **risk tolerance**:
- Who pays for robustness? Owner vs society?
- How much "over-design" is acceptable?

**2026-05-19 application:** MW 1.6 Protective Barrier 同 MW 1.50 Supporting Structure 之間有唔同 redundancy treatment — 兩者都喺 robustness Class 1 territory。

**中英對照 / Cross-cultural note:** 美國傾向 redundancy (post-9/11 NIST recommendations), 歐洲傾向 efficiency (sustainability agenda, EN 15978 LCA framework)。Hong Kong 跟緊 Eurocode 但客戶/業主多數 American-trained → 兩套 vocabulary 時常衝突。

## DG-3: Sequential vs Concurrent Engineering (串行 vs 並行工程)

| Position | Position A: Sequential (Waterfall) | Position B: Concurrent (V-Model / Integrated) |
|---|---|---|
| **過程** | Design → Analysis → Detailing → Construction → Inspection (sequential stages, handoffs) | Design-Construction integrated; BIM-based; clash detection before site |
| **Feedback loops** | Slow, expensive to revisit | Tight, iterative; "design-assist" contractor early |
| **Tool** | 2D CAD, paper drawings | BIM (Revit, Tekla), digital twin |
| **Citation** | PD 1966 (ISO 9001 legacy); traditional contract models | Eastman et al. 2011 (BIM handbook); Sacks et al. 2018 |

**The tension:** Sequential is easier to manage (clear ownership at each stage), but produces rework when late-stage info arrives. Concurrent reduces rework but creates coordination overhead.

# ❓ 10Q — Ten Probing Questions (十個深度問題)

## Q1: 為何 Eurocode 用 partial factors 而美國 ACI 用 strength reduction factor $\phi$？兩種做法數學上係咪等價？

**Detailed answer:**

Eurocode 採用 partial factor approach:
- $\gamma_g = 1.35$ on dead load $G$
- $\gamma_q = 1.50$ on live load $Q$
- $\gamma_c = 1.50$ on concrete $f_{ck}$

驗證式: $\gamma_g G_k + \gamma_q Q_k \leq R_k / \gamma_c$

ACI 318 strength reduction factor approach:
- $U = 1.2D + 1.6L$ (load combination)
- $\phi M_n \geq M_u$ (resistance reduction)
- $\phi = 0.90$ for flexure, $\phi = 0.75$ for shear, $\phi = 0.65$ for compression

**數學上：兩者都係** form: $\gamma_{\text{eff}} \cdot \text{Load} \leq \text{Resistance} / \gamma_{\text{eff}}$

Eurocode 將 factors split across loads 同 materials；ACI 將 factor 集中喺 resistance 一邊。對於簡單 linear case 兩者等價，但對於 mixed loading 模式有 subtle differences。

**關鍵差異：** Eurocode 對唔同 load type (dead vs live vs wind vs seismic) 有唔同 $\gamma$，反映唔同 uncertainty；ACI 對唔同 resistance mechanism (flexure vs shear vs compression) 有唔同 $\phi$，反映唔同 failure mode variability。

引用：MacGregor 1997 §1.5; Bartlett et al. 2003 (ACI 318 design examples); Eurocode 0 §6.

## Q2: Internal staircase 嘅 load path 由 slab 到 foundation，中間經過 stringer、landing beam、column。如果 stringer 同 landing beam 連接 node 出現 construction defect，會唔會 collapse？點樣用 MM-1 (Load Path Continuity) 回答？

**Detailed answer:**

Load path integrity requires: **every node must transfer force without slip**.

如果 stringer-landing beam node 有 defect (e.g., insufficient rebar anchorage):
- Under ULS load $w_u = 11.45$ kN/m², moment demand at node = ~12 kN·m
- 假設 defective node 只能 transfer 5 kN·m → failure initiates
- 但只要 landing beam 仲有 alternative load path (e.g., secondary beam spanning parallel), force 可以 redistribute
- If no alternative path → progressive collapse trigger (MM-2 connection)

**Real case study:** Ronan Point 1968 — gas explosion 破壞咗一個 corner panel，load path disruption 導致成面 wall 倒塌。Source: U.K. Department of the Environment report (1968); Pearson & Delatte 2005.

**Application to MW 1.1:** RSE check 應該 verify:
1. Anchorage length at stringer-landing beam junction ≥ $l_{bd}$ per EC2 §8.4
2. Top reinforcement continuity across junction
3. Minimum tie force per EC2 §7.3 (robustness tie requirement)

如果 check pass，但 construction 出現 un-rebared junction → 設計假設失效 → collapse。

**Conclusion:** Load path mental model 唔止係 design check，仲要包括 **construction quality assurance** (PNAP APP-21 香港 specifically addresses this via ICE regime)。

## Q3: Robot manipulator 嘅 DH parameters 同 structural frame 嘅 node coordinates 有咩 analogy？兩者可以用同一個 mathematical framework 描述嗎？

**Detailed answer:**

**DH parameter analogy:**

| Robot (Craig 2005) | Structural Frame |
|---|---|
| Joint angle $\theta_i$ | Member rotation angle |
| Link length $a_i$ | Member length |
| Link twist $\alpha_i$ | Member cross-section orientation |
| Link offset $d_i$ | Eccentricity at connection |
| Transformation $^{i-1}T_i$ | Local-to-global coordinate transform |

**Equation shared:** Both use $4 \times 4$ homogeneous transformation matrices.

$$T_{\text{frame}} = \prod_{i=1}^{n} ^{i-1}T_i$$

For a structural frame: $T$ transforms member-end forces from local axes to global axes, enabling stiffness matrix assembly.

For a robot: $T$ transforms joint positions to end-effector pose.

**Mathematical equivalence:** Both are **SE(3)** (special Euclidean group) actions. The algebra is identical. The only difference is semantics:
- Robot: $\mathbf{q}$ = joint angles → $\mathbf{x}$ = end-effector pose (forward kinematics)
- Frame: $\mathbf{u}$ = nodal displacements → $\mathbf{F}$ = nodal forces (stiffness method)

**Source:** Craig 2005 Chapter 3; Bathe 1996 *Finite Element Procedures* §6 (structural matrices); Lie group formulation: Murray, Li & Sastry 1994 *A Mathematical Introduction to Robotic Manipulation*.

**2026-05-19 application:** IRE Week 2 Robotics Mechanisms 正好 teaching DH parameters。Structural engineering background 嘅 student 會發現呢個 transformation algebra 已經喺 stiffness method 入面學過 — 只係換咗 context。

## Q4: 點解 Eurocode 要求 minimum reinforcement (e.g., $A_{s,min} = 0.26 f_{ctm} / f_{yk} b_t d$ for beams) 即使 calculation 顯示 zero tension？

**Detailed answer:**

**方程 (per EC2 §9.2.1.1):**

$$A_{s,\min} = \max\left(0.26 \frac{f_{ctm}}{f_{yk}} b_t d,\ 0.0013 b_t d\right)$$

**Reasoning (4 overlapping justifications):**

1. **Crack control:** 如果 calculated reinforcement 太低，受彎构件喺 shrinkage/thermal load 下會 crack。Minimum steel 確保 cracks 分散 (multiple fine cracks) 而非 localized (one wide crack)。Source: Beeby & Narayanan 1995 §4.6; Eurocode 2 commentary (Narayanan & Goodchild 2006).

2. **Robustness tie:** 確保 accidental load scenario 入面有 alternative tensile path。Source: Eurocode 1-1-7 §3.3; NIST 2007.

3. **Brittle failure prevention:** Without minimum steel, plain concrete beam 會 brittle fail (snap)。Minimum steel 確保 ductile flexural failure。Source: Mindess, Young & Darwin 2003 *Concrete* §9.

4. **Construction tolerance:** 避免 zero reinforcement case 喺 real construction (rebar placement error → no steel at critical location)。

**中英對照 / Bilingual insight:**
- 英文: "minimum reinforcement for crack control and ductility"
- 中文: 「最小構造配筋」(構造 = detailing requirement, 唔係 strength requirement)

**2026-05-19 application:** MW 1.1 Internal Staircase — 即使 span moment 細，都要提供 $A_{s,min}$ 喺底面同 top。

## Q5: Reliability index $\beta = 3.8$ 點解係 magic number？改 $\beta = 4.3$ 會點？

**Detailed answer:**

**Equation (Hasofer-Lind 1974):**

$$\beta = \frac{\mu_R - \mu_S}{\sqrt{\sigma_R^2 + \sigma_S^2}}$$

Where $R$ = resistance, $S$ = action effect, both assumed lognormal in Eurocode basis.

**Target values (per Eurocode 0 Annex C, ISO 2394:2015):**

| $\beta$ | $P_f$ per year | Use case |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | $10^{-3}$ | Serviceability limit state |
| 3.8 | $10^{-4}$ | ULS, 50-year reference, normal consequence |
| 4.3 | $10^{-5}$ | ULS, 1-year reference OR high consequence |

**Origin of 3.8:** Practical calibration with existing building stock. Pre-1980 European buildings empirically had $P_f \approx 10^{-4}$ to $10^{-5}$/year for ULS collapse. Eurocode committee adopted $\beta = 3.8$ as compromise between safety and economy. Source: Vrouwenvelder 2002 (JCSS member); Holický 2009.

**Effect of $\beta = 4.3$:**
- $\phi$ factor would increase from 0.90 to ~0.95
- Cross-section size increase ~5-10%
- Cost increase ~5-15%
- Public risk of structural failure decreases by 10×

**Trade-off curve (typical):**

$$\text{Cost} \propto -\ln P_f$$

So going from $P_f = 10^{-4}$ to $P_f = 10^{-5}$ is roughly 1.0 decade → modest cost increase. Going to $10^{-6}$ → diminishing returns.

**Real example:** Lifts in Hong Kong (high consequence) often target $\beta = 4.5$+ because of public risk.

**Citation:** Holický 2009 (reliability basis); JCSS Probabilistic Model Code 2001; Melchers & Beck 2018.

## Q7: Robot manipulator Jacobian 同 structural stiffness matrix 有咩 mathematical analogy？

**Detailed answer:**

**Robot Jacobian (Craig 2005 §5):**

$$\mathbf{v} = J(\mathbf{q}) \dot{\mathbf{q}}$$

Where:
- $\mathbf{v}$ = end-effector linear/angular velocity (6×1 for spatial)
- $J(\mathbf{q})$ = Jacobian (6×n for n-joint robot)
- $\dot{\mathbf{q}}$ = joint velocity (n×1)

**Structural Stiffness Matrix (Bathe 1996 §6):**

$$\mathbf{F} = K \mathbf{u}$$

Where:
- $\mathbf{F}$ = nodal force vector (6n×1 for 3D frame, accounting for DOF per node)
- $K$ = global stiffness matrix (6n × 6n)
- $\mathbf{u}$ = nodal displacement vector (6n×1)

**Singular value connection:**

For both, the **condition number** $\kappa = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}$ reveals:
- Robot: $\kappa$ large → near singularity → high joint velocity for small end-effector motion
- Frame: $\kappa$ large → ill-conditioned → numerical instability OR physically, mechanism (no rigidity)

**Inverse relationship:**
- Robot inverse kinematics: $\dot{\mathbf{q}} = J^{-1} \mathbf{v}$ (when non-singular)
- Frame inverse: $\mathbf{u} = K^{-1} \mathbf{F}$ (when non-singular, but $K^{-1}$ is rarely formed explicitly; instead use LDLT or Cholesky)

**Citation:** Craig 2005; Spong et al. 2006; Bathe 1996; Hughes 2000 *The Finite Element Method*.

**2026-05-19 application:** 結構工程 student 學 stiffness method 之後，再學 robot Jacobian 會發現 "matrix relating joint to end-effector" 嘅 conceptual pattern 重複出現。

## Q8: 點解 Hong Kong 同時使用 Eurocode 同 British Standard (BS)？兩個 code 之間有咩 conflict？

**Detailed answer:**

**Historical timeline:**
- Pre-1997: BS 8110 (concrete), BS 5950 (steel)
- 1997-2010: Transition period, both BS and EC acceptable
- 2010+: Buildings Department pushes Eurocode adoption
- Current: New submissions typically Eurocode, but legacy projects may reference BS

**Specific conflicts:**

| Topic | BS 8110 | Eurocode 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Partial factor on concrete $\gamma_c$ | 1.50 | 1.50 (same) |
| Partial factor on steel $\gamma_s$ | 1.05 | 1.15 |
| Load factor on dead load | 1.4 | 1.35 |
| Load factor on live load | 1.6 | 1.50 |
| Crack width limit (quasi-perm.) | 0.3 mm | 0.4 mm (decompression check for aggressive exposure) |
| Min. link spacing | 0.75d | 0.75d (same) |

**Net effect:** Eurocode 2 typically 5-8% more conservative than BS 8110 for similar input. This means a structure designed to BS 8110 might be slightly under-designed by Eurocode calibration.

**Hong Kong specific:** Buildings Department PNAP APP-21 specifies use of "Codes of Practice and Design Manuals". Currently Eurocodes are listed, but BS 8110/5950 are not formally withdrawn.

**Source:** Hong Kong Buildings Department practice notes; Lo 2010 *Eurocode for Hong Kong* (HKIE publication); Narayanan & Goodchild 2006 (EC2 commentary).

## Q9: 點解 RSE 報告要分 Independent Checking Engineer (ICE) 同原 designer？呢個係咪 sequential engineering 嘅 override？

**Detailed answer:**

**Regulatory basis:** Hong Kong Buildings Ordinance §16(1)(a) requires appointment of **Authorized Person (AP)** 同 **Registered Structural Engineer (RSE)**. PNAP APP-21 (2017 revision) introduces **Independent Checking Engineer (ICE)** regime for specific cases.

- Hospital buildings
- Buildings > 30 storeys
- Buildings with transfer structures
- Major alteration works

- Verifies ULS, SLS, robustness, durability, fire
- Issues ICE certificate
- Cannot be same person/team as original designer

**Rationale (4 reasons):**

1. **Human error mitigation:** Cognitive bias in original designer (sunk cost, anchoring on first solution) — fresh eyes catch errors. Source: Sacks et al. 2018 BIM handbook; Reason 2000 *Human Error*; Lovallo & Kahneman 2003.

2. **Accountability diffusion:** Original RSE 唔再係 sole bearer — ICE co-signs.

3. **Code compliance audit:** Independent verification that latest code revision applied.

4. **Insurance/liability:** ICE indemnity insurance separate from original RSE.

**Is this sequential engineering? Yes AND no.**
- Sequential in execution (ICE reviews after original design)
- BUT conceptually concurrent in philosophy (two independent reasoning processes)

**Tension with BIM/concurrent:** BIM promises clash detection during design, theoretically reducing need for ICE. But PNAP APP-21 still requires ICE for high-risk buildings — **regulatory conservatism**.

## Q10: Robot 同 structure 之間最 surprising 嘅 analogy 係咩？由呢個 analogy 可以 derive 出咩 cross-disciplinary insight？

**Detailed answer:**

**The surprising analogy: Control = Structural Stabilization**

- **Robot control** (e.g., computed torque, feedback linearization): $\mathbf{u} = M(\mathbf{q})\ddot{\mathbf{q}}_d + C(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}})\dot{\mathbf{q}} + G(\mathbf{q})$
- **Structural stabilization** (active bracing, mass damper): $F = M \ddot{x} + C \dot{x} + K x + U_{active}$

兩者都係 **second-order ODE with control input**.

**Cross-disciplinary insight (3 levels):**

1. **Stability analysis:** Lyapunov stability theory (robot) ↔ Routh-Hurwitz criteria (structural dynamics). Same math.

2. **Active structural control:** TMD (tuned mass damper) for skyscrapers (e.g., Taipei 101) 跟 same control theory as robot manipulator. Source: Soong & Spencer 2002 (active structural control); Craig 2005.

3. **Optimization:** Robot trajectory optimization (Pontryagin's principle) ↔ Structural optimization (minimum weight design).

**Concrete example — vibration suppression:**
- A robot arm with flexible link → can use **same** state-space model as a flexible structure
- Sensor/actuator placement problem identical (where to put accelerometer on robot arm? Where to put TMD on building?)

**Final synthesis:** The future structural engineer should learn control theory. The future roboticist should learn structural dynamics. **2026-05-19 IRE bootcamp** is positioned exactly at this intersection.

## DD-1: Limit State Design 嘅 Bayesian 後驗更新 (Bayesian Updating in Limit State)

### 中文版

傳統 limit state 設計入面，所有 uncertainty 都係 **prior** (事前) 估計。Eurocode 用 partial factor $\gamma_g = 1.35$、$\gamma_q = 1.50$ 等，反映 prior uncertainty in $G$ 同 $Q$。

但係 construction 完成後，**真實 concrete strength** 可以由 cube test 得知 (e.g., mean $f_c = 35$ MPa vs characteristic $f_{ck} = 25$ MPa, 假設 COV 0.15)。咁樣可以 **Bayesian update** resistance distribution:

$$f(R|D) \propto f(D|R) \cdot f(R)$$

Where $R$ = resistance, $D$ = test data.

Equation: posterior COV of $R$ after $n$ cube tests:

$$\sigma_{R,\text{post}}^2 = \frac{1}{\frac{n}{\sigma_{R,\text{prior}}^2} + \frac{1}{\sigma_{\text{test}}^2}}$$

If $n$ large, $\sigma_{R,\text{post}} \to \sigma_{\text{test}}$ (test uncertainty dominates).

**應用 (Application):**

如果 cube test results 顯示 $f_{cm} = 40$ MPa (much higher than $f_{ck} = 25$)，RSE 可以 argue:
$$\beta_{\text{actual}} = \frac{\mu_R^{\text{updated}} - \mu_S}{\sqrt{(\sigma_R^{\text{updated}})^2 + \sigma_S^2}} > \beta_{\text{target}}$$

即係 real reliability index 已經高過 target → 可以 reduce reinforcement (subject to code minimum)。

**Hong Kong practice:** Buildings Department 接受 cube test-based capacity reassessment per PNAP APP-21 (但需要 ICE concurrence)。

**Citation:** Ang & Tang 2007 *Probability Concepts in Engineering*; Melchers & Beck 2018; Bartlett & MacGregor 1996 (concrete strength evaluation)。

### English Version

In traditional limit state design, all uncertainties are treated as **prior** estimates. Eurocode's partial factors $\gamma_g = 1.35$, $\gamma_q = 1.50$ encode prior uncertainty in dead load $G$ and imposed load $Q$.

After construction, **actual concrete strength** can be measured via cube tests (e.g., mean $f_c = 35$ MPa vs characteristic $f_{ck} = 25$ MPa, assuming COV 0.15). This enables **Bayesian updating** of the resistance distribution:

$$f(R|D) \propto f(D|R) \cdot f(R)$$

The posterior COV of $R$ after $n$ cube tests:

$$\sigma_{R,\text{post}}^2 = \frac{1}{\frac{n}{\sigma_{R,\text{prior}}^2} + \frac{1}{\sigma_{\text{test}}^2}}$$

**Application:** If cube tests show $f_{cm} = 40$ MPa (much higher than $f_{ck} = 25$), RSE can argue that actual reliability index:

$$\beta_{\text{actual}} = \frac{\mu_R^{\text{updated}} - \mu_S}{\sqrt{(\sigma_R^{\text{updated}})^2 + \sigma_S^2}} > \beta_{\text{target}}$$

Exceeds target → reinforcement reduction possible (subject to code minimums).

**Hong Kong practice:** Buildings Department accepts cube-based capacity reassessment per PNAP APP-21 (with ICE concurrence).

**Reference:** Ang & Tang 2007; Melchers & Beck 2018; Bartlett & MacGregor 1996.

## DD-2: Forward Kinematics via Denavit-Hartenberg (DH) Parameters

### 中文版

DH convention (Denavit & Hartenberg 1955) 提供 systematic 方法嚟定義 robot link geometry。四個參數描述每個 joint:

| 參數 | 物理意義 |
|---|---|
| $\theta_i$ | Joint $i$ 嘅 rotation angle (revolute) |
| $d_i$ | Joint $i$ 嘅 translation (prismatic) |
| $a_i$ | Link $i$ 嘅 length |
| $\alpha_i$ | Link $i$ 嘅 twist angle |

**Transformation matrix** 由 joint $i-1$ 到 joint $i$:

$$^{i-1}T_i(\theta_i, d_i, a_i, \alpha_i) = R_z(\theta_i) T_z(d_i) T_x(a_i) R_x(\alpha_i)$$

展開:

$$^{i-1}T_i = \begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta_i & -\sin\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & \sin\theta_i\sin\alpha_i & a_i\cos\theta_i \\
\sin\theta_i & \cos\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & -\cos\theta_i\sin\alpha_i & a_i\sin\theta_i \\
0 & \sin\alpha_i & \cos\alpha_i & d_i \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}$$

**Forward kinematics** 係 simple multiplication:

$$T_{\text{base}}^{\text{end}} = \prod_{i=1}^{n} ^{i-1}T_i(\theta_i)$$

對於 6-DOF PUMA robot (n=6), 計算 $4\times4$ matrix 嘅 12 entries (top-left 3×3 rotation + top 3×1 translation)。

**Inverse problem:** 畀 end-effector pose $(x, y, z, \phi, \theta, \psi)$，搵 $\theta_1, ..., \theta_6$。一般有多個 solution (multiplicity), 需要 inverse kinematics solver (analytical for n≤6 with simple geometry; numerical Jacobian-based for complex)。

**應用 (Application):** ROS (Robot Operating System) 入面 `tf` library 用 exactly 呢個 formulation。MoveIt! motion planner 亦都 build on DH。

**Reference:** Craig 2005; Spong, Hutchinson & Vidyasagar 2006; Denavit & Hartenberg 1955; Paul 1981; Lynch & Park 2017 *Modern Robotics*。

### English Version

The DH convention (Denavit & Hartenberg 1955) provides a systematic method to define robot link geometry. Four parameters describe each joint:

| Parameter | Physical meaning |
|---|---|
| $\theta_i$ | Joint $i$ rotation angle (revolute) |
| $d_i$ | Joint $i$ translation (prismatic) |
| $a_i$ | Link $i$ length |
| $\alpha_i$ | Link $i$ twist angle |

**Transformation matrix** from joint $i-1$ to joint $i$:

$$^{i-1}T_i = \begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta_i & -\sin\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & \sin\theta_i\sin\alpha_i & a_i\cos\theta_i \\
\sin\theta_i & \cos\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & -\cos\theta_i\sin\alpha_i & a_i\sin\theta_i \\
0 & \sin\alpha_i & \cos\alpha_i & d_i \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}$$

**Forward kinematics** is matrix multiplication:

$$T_{\text{base}}^{\text{end}} = \prod_{i=1}^{n} ^{i-1}T_i(\theta_i)$$

**Inverse problem:** Given end-effector pose $(x, y, z, \phi, \theta, \psi)$, find $\theta_1, ..., \theta_6$. Generally multiple solutions; requires inverse kinematics solver.

**Application:** ROS `tf` library uses this formulation; MoveIt! motion planner builds on DH.

**Reference:** Craig 2005; Spong et al. 2006; Lynch & Park 2017.

## DD-3: Reliability Index 與 Partial Factor 嘅 calibration (How $\gamma$ values are derived)

### 中文版

Eurocode 嘅 partial factor **唔係 arbitrarily chosen** — 佢係 calibrated to achieve target reliability $\beta = 3.8$ over 50-year reference period。

**Step 1: Load model**

Action effect $S$ = $G$ + $Q$, where:
- $G \sim \text{Normal}(\mu_G, \sigma_G)$, COV = 0.10 for dead load
- $Q \sim \text{Gumbel}(\mu_Q, \sigma_Q)$, COV = 0.35 for imposed load (per JCSS)

**Step 2: Resistance model**

$R = b \cdot f_c \cdot a_s \cdot f_y$, with:
- $f_c \sim \text{Lognormal}$, COV = 0.15 (in-situ concrete)
- $f_y \sim \text{Beta}$, COV = 0.05 (steel yield)
- Geometric COV = 0.05

**Step 3: First-order reliability method (FORM)**

Find **design point** on failure surface ($R = S$):

$$\mathbf{u}^* = \frac{\nabla g(\mathbf{u})}{\|\nabla g(\mathbf{u})\|} \cdot \beta$$

Where $g(\mathbf{u}) = \ln R - \ln S$ (limit state function in standard normal space).

**Step 4: Derive partial factors**

From design point, sensitivities:

$$\alpha_G = \frac{\partial \beta / \partial \sigma_G}{\text{norm}}, \quad \alpha_Q = \frac{\partial \beta / \partial \sigma_Q}{\text{norm}}$$

Then design values:

$$G_d = \mu_G (1 + \alpha_G \cdot V_G \cdot \beta), \quad Q_d = \mu_Q (1 + \alpha_Q \cdot V_Q \cdot \beta)$$

Equivalent partial factor:

$$\gamma_G = G_d / G_k, \quad \gamma_Q = Q_d / Q_k$$

For typical residential loads, $\gamma_G \approx 1.35$ 同 $\gamma_Q \approx 1.50$。

**Note:** 結果 depends on **load ratio** $Q/G$. For low $Q/G$ ratio (heavy structure), $\gamma_G$ slightly lower; for high $Q/G$ (light roof), $\gamma_Q$ dominates. Eurocode 用 simplified constant factors。

**Citation:** Vrouwenvelder 2002; Holický 2009; JCSS PMC 2001; Eurocode 0 Annex C; Faber 2012 *Risk Assessment in Engineering*.

### English Version

Eurocode partial factors are **not arbitrary** — they are calibrated to achieve target reliability $\beta = 3.8$ over 50-year reference.

**Step 1: Load model**
- $G \sim \text{Normal}$, COV = 0.10
- $Q \sim \text{Gumbel}$, COV = 0.35

**Step 2: Resistance model**
- $f_c \sim \text{Lognormal}$, COV = 0.15
- $f_y \sim \text{Beta}$, COV = 0.05
- Geometric COV = 0.05

**Step 3: FORM**
$$\mathbf{u}^* = \frac{\nabla g(\mathbf{u})}{\|\nabla g(\mathbf{u})\|} \cdot \beta$$

**Step 4: Partial factor derivation**
$$G_d = \mu_G (1 + \alpha_G V_G \beta)$$
$$\gamma_G = G_d / G_k \approx 1.35$$

Result depends on $Q/G$ ratio. For low ratio: heavy structure; for high ratio: light roof.

**Reference:** Vrouwenvelder 2002; Holický 2009; Faber 2012.

## DD-4: Robot Workspace 同 Structural Member Force Capacity

### 中文版

**Robot workspace** (Craig 2005 §3.4) = 末端執行器可以到達嘅所有位置集合。對於 2-link planar arm:

$$x = l_1 \cos\theta_1 + l_2 \cos(\theta_1 + \theta_2)$$
$$y = l_1 \sin\theta_1 + l_2 \sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2)$$

Workspace boundary (when arm fully extended, $\theta_2 = 0$): outer circle radius $l_1 + l_2$. Inner boundary (when arm fully folded, $\theta_2 = \pi$): circle radius $|l_1 - l_2|$.

**Reachable workspace** (point reachable in any orientation): annulus.
**Dexterous workspace** (point reachable in all orientations): single point if any.

**Structural analogy:** 一個 structural member 嘅 force capacity:

$$M_{Rd} = A_s f_{yd} z \quad \text{(tension-controlled flexure)}$$

Capacity envelope：M-N interaction diagram。

**Surprising analogy:**

| Robot | Structure |
|---|---|
| Workspace = reachable region | Capacity envelope = feasible force region |
| Singularity = boundary of workspace | Limit state = boundary of capacity |
| Inverse kinematics = find $\theta$ for given $(x,y)$ | Design = find $A_s$ for given $(M, N)$ |
| Trajectory planning = avoid singularities | Design optimization = stay within capacity envelope |

**Both problems are:**
- Constrained optimization
- Feasibility region defined by algebra
- Multiple solutions typically (inverse problem non-unique)

**Industrial implication:** 結構工程師學習 robot kinematics 之後，會發現自己一直喺做 "inverse kinematics" — 畀定 demand ($M, N, V$)，搵 capacity ($A_s, b, d$)，呢個過程同 robot inverse kinematics 數學上同構。

**Citation:** Craig 2005; Spong et al. 2006; MacGregor 1997.

**Robustness definition** (per Eurocode 1-1-7):

**Two design strategies:**

1. **Tie force method** (prescriptive): 確保 minimum horizontal ties (typically $T_i = 0.4 \times \text{characteristic floor load}$) 同 vertical ties ($V_{tie} = \text{max column load}$)。

**Equation — bridge load after column loss (energy balance, Izzuddin et al. 2008):**

$$P_{\text{bridge}} = \frac{8 M_{p,\text{beam}}}{L}$$

PNAP APP-21 同 Code of Practice for Structural Use of Concrete 2013 (currently under revision) 都要求:

1. Horizontal ties at floor levels (每 slab + 每 beam)
2. Vertical ties through columns
3. Key elements identified (cannot be removed without disproportionate collapse)

### SL-1: Internal Staircase (MW 1.1) — Calculate ULS Moment Capacity for 150 mm slab

**Question:** 對於 Internal Staircase (MW 1.1, span 3.0 m, thickness 150 mm, design load 11.45 kN/m² ULS), calculate moment capacity assuming C30 concrete + 10 mm @ 150 c/c bottom steel.

- $f_{ck} = 30$ MPa (C30), $f_{cd} = f_{ck}/\gamma_c = 30/1.50 = 20$ MPa

### SL-3: Reliability Index Calculation

**Question:** 對於 resistance $R \sim \text{Lognormal}(\mu_R = 100, \sigma_R = 15)$, action effect $S \sim \text{Normal}(\mu_S = 60, \sigma_S = 10)$, compute $\beta$.

**Solution:**

For lognormal $R$:
- $\xi_R = \sqrt{\ln(1 + (\sigma_R/\mu_R)^2)} = \sqrt{\ln(1 + 0.0225)} = \sqrt{0.0222} = 0.149$
- $\lambda_R = \ln \mu_R - \xi_R^2/2 = \ln 100 - 0.0111 = 4.594$

FORM (Hasofer-Lind):

Failure surface $g(R, S) = R - S = 0$ (linear in this case, exact solution possible)

Equivalent normal transformation: $R' = \Phi^{-1}(F_R(R^*))$, etc.

Linear case shortcut: For $g = R - S$ with both normal:
$$\beta = \frac{\mu_R - \mu_S}{\sqrt{\sigma_R^2 + \sigma_S^2}} = \frac{100 - 60}{\sqrt{15^2 + 10^2}} = \frac{40}{\sqrt{325}} = \frac{40}{18.03} = 2.22$$

But $R$ is lognormal, not normal. Need transformation:

$$R^* = \mu_R (1 - V_R \cdot 0.5) \approx 100(1 - 0.15 \cdot 0.5) = 92.5$$

Approximation: $\beta \approx (\mu_R - \mu_S) / \sqrt{(\sigma_R')^2 + \sigma_S^2}$ where $\sigma_R'$ accounts for lognormal shape.

Iterative FORM:
1. Start with $\beta_0 = (\mu_R - \mu_S)/\sqrt{\sigma_R^2 + \sigma_S^2} = 2.22$
2. Design point: $R^* = \mu_R - \alpha_R \sigma_R \beta$, $S^* = \mu_S + \alpha_S \sigma_S \beta$
3. $\alpha_R = \sigma_R/\sqrt{\sigma_R^2 + \sigma_S^2} = 0.832$, $\alpha_S = 10/18.03 = 0.555$
4. $R^* = 100 - 0.832 \times 15 \times 2.22 = 72.3$, $S^* = 60 + 0.555 \times 10 \times 2.22 = 72.3$ ✓ (consistent)
5. Transform to standard normal: $u_R = \Phi^{-1}(F_R(R^*)) = \Phi^{-1}(F_R(72.3))$

For lognormal $R$ with $\mu = 100$, $\sigma = 15$: $F_R(72.3) \approx 0.13$, $u_R \approx -1.13$

Recompute $\beta = \sqrt{u_R^2 + u_S^2} = \sqrt{1.13^2 + 1.13^2} = 1.60$

**Answer:** $\beta \approx 1.60$ (lognormal $R$ gives lower $\beta$ than normal approximation)

**Probability of failure:** $P_f = \Phi(-1.60) = 5.5\%$

**Conclusion:** $\beta = 1.60 < 3.8$ → **NOT adequate**. Need stronger $R$ or lower $S$.

**Citation:** Melchers & Beck 2018; Hasofer & Lind 1974.

### SL-5: Inverse Kinematics for 2R planar arm

**Question:** Given $l_1 = 1$ m, $l_2 = 1$ m, end-effector target $(x, y) = (1.5, 0.5)$ m, find $\theta_1, \theta_2$.

**Solution:**

Compute $\theta_2$:
$$\cos\theta_2 = \frac{x^2 + y^2 - l_1^2 - l_2^2}{2 l_1 l_2} = \frac{1.5^2 + 0.5^2 - 1 - 1}{2 \times 1 \times 1} = \frac{2.25 + 0.25 - 2}{2} = \frac{0.5}{2} = 0.25$$

$$\theta_2 = \arccos(0.25) = \pm 75.5°$$

Compute $\theta_1$:
$$\theta_1 = \arctan2(y, x) - \arctan2(l_2 \sin\theta_2, l_1 + l_2 \cos\theta_2)$$

For $\theta_2 = +75.5°$ (elbow up):
- $\sin 75.5° = 0.968$, $\cos 75.5° = 0.25$
- $l_2 \sin\theta_2 = 0.968$, $l_1 + l_2 \cos\theta_2 = 1.25$
- $\arctan2(0.968, 1.25) = 37.8°$
- $\arctan2(0.5, 1.5) = 18.4°$
- $\theta_1 = 18.4° - 37.8° = -19.4°$

For $\theta_2 = -75.5°$ (elbow down):
- $\sin(-75.5°) = -0.968$, $\cos(-75.5°) = 0.25$
- $l_2 \sin\theta_2 = -0.968$
- $\arctan2(-0.968, 1.25) = -37.8°$
- $\theta_1 = 18.4° - (-37.8°) = 56.2°$

**Answer:**
- Elbow up: $(\theta_1, \theta_2) = (-19.4°, +75.5°)$
- Elbow down: $(\theta_1, \theta_2) = (+56.2°, -75.5°)$

Two valid solutions; robot controller typically chooses the closer one to current configuration.

**Citation:** Craig 2005 §3.4; Spong et al. 2006.

### SL-6: Bayesian Update of Concrete Strength

**Question:** Prior: $f_c \sim \text{Normal}(\mu = 30, \sigma = 4.5)$ MPa. After 5 cube tests: sample mean $\bar{x} = 35$ MPa, sample std $s = 3.0$ MPa. Find posterior distribution.

**Solution:**

Prior: $\mu_0 = 30$, $\sigma_0 = 4.5$, $n_0 = \sigma_0^2 / \sigma_{\text{hyper}}^2$ (effective sample size, treat as infinite for diffuse prior)

Likelihood (sample mean from 5 tests): $\bar{x} \sim \text{Normal}(\mu_{\text{true}}, \sigma/\sqrt{5}) = \text{Normal}(\mu_{\text{true}}, 3.0/\sqrt{5} = 1.34)$

Posterior (conjugate normal update):

For diffuse prior (treating prior variance as much larger than likelihood variance):
$$\mu_{\text{post}} \approx \frac{\mu_0 / \sigma_0^2 + \bar{x} / \sigma_{\bar{x}}^2}{1/\sigma_0^2 + 1/\sigma_{\bar{x}}^2}$$

Wait, need proper conjugate update. With known variance:

$$\mu_{\text{post}} = \frac{\mu_0 \cdot (1/\sigma_0^2) + \bar{x} \cdot (1/\sigma_{\bar{x}}^2)}{1/\sigma_0^2 + 1/\sigma_{\bar{x}}^2} = \frac{30/20.25 + 35/1.80}{1/20.25 + 1/1.80} = \frac{1.481 + 19.444}{0.0494 + 0.556} = \frac{20.93}{0.605} = 34.6 \text{ MPa}$$

$$\sigma_{\text{post}}^2 = \frac{1}{1/\sigma_0^2 + 1/\sigma_{\bar{x}}^2} = \frac{1}{0.0494 + 0.556} = 1.65$$
$$\sigma_{\text{post}} = 1.29 \text{ MPa}$$

**Answer:**
- Prior: $f_c \sim N(30, 4.5)$ MPa
- Posterior: $f_c \sim N(34.6, 1.29)$ MPa

**Implication:** Updated mean increases (test confirms higher strength), uncertainty reduces 3.5×.

**RSE decision:** Since updated $f_{cm} = 34.6 > f_{ck} = 25$ by margin, can argue design is robust. But must still meet code minimum reinforcement.

**Citation:** Ang & Tang 2007; Bartlett & MacGregor 1996.

### SL-7: Deflection Check (SLS) for Slab

**Question:** MW 1.1 slab, span 3.0 m, quasi-permanent load $w_{qp} = 6.5$ kN/m² (assume), $E_c = 33$ GPa, $f_{ck} = 30$ MPa. Check deflection.

**Solution:**

For quasi-permanent load, creep factor $\phi = 2.0$ (long-term):

Effective modulus:
$$E_{c,\text{eff}} = \frac{E_c}{1 + \phi} = \frac{33}{1 + 2} = 11 \text{ GPa}$$

Second moment of area (uncracked, gross section):
$$I = b d^3 / 12 = 1000 \times 120^3 / 12 = 144 \times 10^6 \text{ mm}^4 = 1.44 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}^4$$

Deflection (simply supported, uniform load):
$$\delta = \frac{5 w_{qp} L^4}{384 E_{c,\text{eff}} I} = \frac{5 \times 6.5 \times 3.0^4}{384 \times 11 \times 10^9 \times 1.44 \times 10^{-4}}$$

$$= \frac{5 \times 6.5 \times 81}{384 \times 1.584 \times 10^6} = \frac{2632.5}{6.082 \times 10^8} = 4.33 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m} = 0.00433 \text{ mm}$$

Hmm, this is unrealistically small. Let me reconsider — for a 1-meter wide strip, $b = 1$ m, not 1 mm. Recompute:

$$I = 1.0 \times 0.12^3 / 12 = 1.44 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}^4$$

$$\delta = \frac{5 \times 6500 \times 3.0^4}{384 \times 11 \times 10^9 \times 1.44 \times 10^{-4}}$$

$$= \frac{5 \times 6500 \times 81}{6.082 \times 10^8} = \frac{2,632,500}{6.082 \times 10^8} = 4.33 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m} = 4.33 \text{ mm}$$

**Limit:** $L/250 = 3000/250 = 12$ mm

**Check:** $\delta = 4.33$ mm $< 12$ mm ✓ **Adequate**

**Note:** This is simplified; Eurocode 2 §7.4 has more detailed approach (cracked/uncracked interpolation).

**Citation:** Eurocode 2 §7.4; Beeby & Narayanan 1995.
