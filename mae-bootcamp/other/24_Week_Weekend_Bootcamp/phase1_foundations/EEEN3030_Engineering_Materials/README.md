# EEEN3030 Engineering Materials

**Phase 1 | Priority: ★★★**  
**Why critical**: Material selection for rigid links, soft actuators, sensors and structures.

---

## 1. 5 Core Mental Models Every Expert Shares

1. **Structure Determines Properties** — Crystal structure, microstructure and defects control behaviour.
2. **Processing–Structure–Properties–Performance Chain** — Every decision ripples through the chain.
3. **Trade-offs are Inevitable** — Strength vs toughness, stiffness vs density, cost vs performance.
4. **Defects are Often Useful** — Dislocations enable plasticity; dopants enable semiconductors.
5. **Selection is Multi-Objective** — Ashby charts formalise the compromise.

---

## 2. 3 Places Experts Fundamentally Disagree

### Disagreement 1: Metals-First vs Broader Materials Classes Early
- Metals dominate traditional design. Polymers, ceramics and composites are equally important today.

### Disagreement 2: Microstructure Emphasis vs Continuum Properties
- Microstructure explains “why”. Continuum properties are what designers use day-to-day.

### Disagreement 3: How Much Chemistry is Required
- Some chemistry is essential for bonding and corrosion. Heavy chemistry displaces mechanics.

## 3. 10 Questions that Distinguish Deep Understanding

1. Why does cold working increase strength but decrease ductility?
2. What is the physical origin of yield strength in a pure metal?
3. How does glass-transition temperature affect a polymer soft actuator?
4. Why are ceramics strong in compression but weak in tension?
5. What does a phase diagram tell you that a single composition cannot?
6. How would you choose between aluminium, titanium and CFRP for a robot arm link?
7. Why do residual stresses arise in welding or additive manufacturing?
8. Difference between hardness and strength, and when each matters more?
9. How does diffusion coefficient enter heat-treatment decisions?
10. In soft robotics, why is Shore hardness / Young’s modulus of Ecoflex often more critical than ultimate tensile strength?
