# ENGG5404 Micromachining and MEMS

**Phase 3 | Priority: ★★★**  
**Why critical**: Micro-scale sensors and actuators — the physical basis of many modern robot sensors and emerging micro-robots.

---

## 1. 5 Core Mental Models Every Expert Shares

1. **Scaling Laws Change Everything** — Surface forces, viscosity and electrostatics dominate at small scales; inertia becomes less important.
2. **Process Defines Geometry** — What you can etch, deposit and release determines what devices are possible.
3. **Residual Stress and Stiction are First-Class Problems** — Microstructures fail in ways macroscopic parts rarely do.
4. **Integration with Electronics is the Product** — A MEMS sensor is only useful when readout, packaging and calibration are solved.
5. **Batch Fabrication is the Economic Engine** — Cost drops with volume only if the process is compatible with semiconductor-style manufacturing.

---

## 2. 3 Places Experts Fundamentally Disagree

### Disagreement 1: Surface Micromachining vs Bulk Micromachining as the Default
- Surface: better integration with CMOS. Bulk: thicker structures, different mechanical properties.

### Disagreement 2: How Much Process Detail vs Device Physics
- Process depth is needed to design manufacturable devices. Device physics is needed to use them.

### Disagreement 3: MEMS vs Macro Sensors for Robotics
- MEMS: small, cheap, low power. Macro: sometimes still better performance, robustness or ease of integration.
