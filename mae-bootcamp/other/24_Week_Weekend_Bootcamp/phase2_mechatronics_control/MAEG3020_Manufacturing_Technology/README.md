# MAEG3020 Manufacturing Technology

**Phase 2 | Priority: ★★★★**  
**Why critical**: How parts are actually made — process capability, tolerances, and design for manufacture that constrain every robot design.

---

## 1. 5 Core Mental Models Every Expert Shares

1. **Process Capability Limits Design** — You cannot specify what the process cannot reliably produce. Tolerance stacks and process variation are first-class constraints.
2. **Material Removal / Addition / Forming are Different Worlds** — Subtractive, additive and formative processes have fundamentally different geometric, material and cost implications.
3. **Tolerance is Money** — Tighter tolerances raise cost non-linearly. The right tolerance is the loosest one that still meets function.
4. **Design for Manufacture and Assembly (DFMA)** — The best design is the one that can be made and assembled robustly at scale.
5. **Metrology Closes the Loop** — You only know what you can measure. Inspection strategy is part of the manufacturing system.

---

## 2. 3 Places Experts Fundamentally Disagree

### Disagreement 1: Additive vs Traditional Manufacturing as Default
- **Additive camp**: geometric freedom, rapid iteration, on-demand spares. **Traditional camp**: superior material properties, surface finish, cost at volume, and established process control.

### Disagreement 2: How Early to Teach Statistical Process Control
- **Early**: variation is the reality of manufacturing. **Later**: first master the physics of processes.

### Disagreement 3: In-house vs Outsourced Manufacturing Knowledge
- **In-house**: deep process understanding enables better design. **Outsourced**: focus on system integration and supplier management.

## 3. 10 Questions that Distinguish Deep Understanding

1. Why does tightening a tolerance from ±0.1 mm to ±0.01 mm often more than double the part cost?
2. What is the difference between accuracy and precision in a manufacturing process, and why does it matter for robot links?
3. When would you choose CNC machining over 3D printing for a structural robot component?
4. How does the concept of a process capability index (Cpk) influence design decisions?
5. Explain why additive manufacturing of metals often requires significant post-processing.
6. What geometric features are inherently expensive or impossible for certain processes?
7. How would you decide the datum scheme for a multi-feature robot base plate?
8. Why is springback a critical issue in sheet-metal forming of robot covers or brackets?
9. In the context of a soft-gripper mold, which manufacturing process choices most affect the final actuator performance?
10. How does Design for Assembly change the number and type of fasteners you use in a robot?
