## 1. 5 Core Mental Models Every Expert Shares

1. **Value is Defined by the Customer** — Technical elegance does not equal market value.
2. **Cash Flow Timing Matters** — Present value, payback and risk-adjusted returns guide decisions.
3. **Innovation is a Process** — Idea → validation → scaling, with many failure points.
4. **Competitive Advantage Must be Sustainable** — Technology alone is often easy to copy.
5. **Teams and Execution Dominate Ideas** — A mediocre idea well executed beats a brilliant idea poorly delivered.

---

## 2. 3 Places Experts Fundamentally Disagree

### Disagreement 1: Technology-Push vs Market-Pull Innovation
- Push: breakthroughs create new markets. Pull: clear customer needs guide development.

### Disagreement 2: How Much Business Content Engineers Need
- Essential for leadership and startups. Can dilute deep technical training if overdone.

### Disagreement 3: Lean Startup vs Heavy Upfront Design in Hardware
- Lean: fast learning with minimal viable products. Hardware often needs more upfront commitment than software.

## 3. 10 Questions that Distinguish Deep Understanding

1. How would you estimate the cost of goods for a soft-gripper product at low volume?
2. What is the difference between a prototype and a minimum viable product in hardware?
3. How do you identify the early adopters for a new robotic handling system?
4. What intellectual-property strategy makes sense for an open-source-leaning robotics project?
5. How does unit economics change when moving from one-off prototypes to small-batch production?
6. What non-technical risks most often kill hardware startups?
7. How would you structure a simple business case for deploying warehouse robots in a local SME?
8. What metrics would an investor or manager use to judge your hybrid system project?
9. How does open-source hardware change the competitive landscape?
10. Given your current skills, what is the most plausible path from this bootcamp project toward real-world impact?
