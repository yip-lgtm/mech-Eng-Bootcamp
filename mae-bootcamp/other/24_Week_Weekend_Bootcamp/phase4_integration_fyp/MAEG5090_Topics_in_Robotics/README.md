## 1. 5 Core Mental Models Every Expert Shares

1. **The Field Moves Fast** — Survey and primary papers are the living curriculum.
2. **Problem Formulation is Half the Contribution** — Clear questions, metrics and baselines define good research.
3. **Reproducibility and Open Artifacts Matter** — Code, data and hardware details determine whether others can build on the work.
4. **Integration of Perception, Planning and Control is the Hard Part** — Isolated modules are easier; the interfaces are where systems fail.
5. **Application Constraints Drive Research Choices** — Warehouse, medical, space and domestic robots optimise different objectives.

---

## 2. 3 Places Experts Fundamentally Disagree

### Disagreement 1: Breadth of Survey vs Depth on One Topic
- Breadth gives orientation. Depth produces competence and possible contribution.

### Disagreement 2: Simulation-Only Research vs Hardware Validation
- Simulation enables scale and speed. Hardware remains the final test for many claims.

### Disagreement 3: Incremental vs Moonshot Research Goals
- Incremental work is publishable and cumulative. Moonshots can redefine the field but often fail.

## 3. 10 Questions that Distinguish Deep Understanding

1. How do you judge whether a robotics paper’s claims are well supported by its experiments?
2. What constitutes a strong baseline for a new manipulation or locomotion method?
3. How would you turn an open problem in soft gripping into a concrete, measurable research question?
4. What are the typical failure modes of sim-to-real transfer in recent papers?
5. How do you decide which related work is essential to cite and compare against?
6. What open datasets or benchmarks are most relevant to warehouse-style picking?
7. How does the evaluation of a soft robot differ from that of a rigid industrial arm?
8. What would a minimal publishable contribution look like based on your hybrid system?
9. How do you balance novelty against thoroughness in a semester-long topics project?
10. Which current research direction (soft robotics, learning, planning, human–robot interaction) is the best fit for your next step?
