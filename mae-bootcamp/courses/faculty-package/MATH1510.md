## 5MM — 5 Mental Models / 5 個核心心智模型

### MM1. Limit and Continuity / 極限與連續

**Formal definition (Weierstrass 1872):** A function $f$ is continuous at $a$ iff
$$\forall\,\varepsilon>0,\;\exists\,\delta>0:\;|x-a|<\delta \;\Rightarrow\; |f(x)-f(a)|<\varepsilon$$
Equivalently, $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$ (Heine 1872). The $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ formulation, introduced by Weierstrass in his Berlin lectures and published posthumously, eliminated the geometric/Newtonian "infinitesimals" and placed analysis on a rigorous foundation (Rudin 1976, *Principles of Mathematical Analysis*).

**Engineering interpretation:** A robot joint commanded to "move smoothly" from $30°$ to $31°$ requires the position function to be continuous in time; if the controller produces a discontinuous velocity, mechanical wear and audible chatter result. Sensor noise is non-continuous at the sample level but the underlying physical signal is continuous — hence low-pass filtering is justified.

**Historical anchor:** The limit concept was implicit in Newton (1687, *Principia*) and Leibniz (1684, *Nova Methodus*), but rigorously formalized by Cauchy (1821, *Cours d'Analyse*) and Weierstrass (1872).

### MM2. Derivative as Rate of Change / 導數是變化率

**Definition:**
$$f'(x) \;\equiv\; \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h} \;=\; \frac{df}{dx}$$
Geometrically, $f'(a)$ is the slope of the unique tangent line through $(a, f(a))$ (Lagrange 1797). The chain rule
$$\frac{d}{dt}f(g(t)) = f'(g(t))\cdot g'(t)$$
is the workhorse behind backpropagation in neural networks (Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams 1986) and sensitivity analysis in control design (Franklin, Powell & Emami-Naeini 2019).

**Engineering applications:**
- Motor velocity from encoder: $v = \dfrac{d\theta}{dt}$
- Joint acceleration: $a = \dfrac{d^2\theta}{dt^2}$
- Power dissipation: $P = \dfrac{dW}{dt}$

**Key references:** Stewart (2016, *Calculus*, 8th ed.); Spivak (1994, *Calculus*, 3rd ed.); Thomas' Calculus (Hass, Heil & Weir 2018).

### MM3. Integral as Accumulation / 積分是累積

**Riemann definition (Riemann 1867):**
$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\Delta x$$
**Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (Barrow 1669, Newton 1687, Leibniz 1693):**
$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)\quad\text{where}\quad F'(x)=f(x)$$

**Engineering meaning:** "Accumulation over time/space." Examples:
- **Displacement** = $\int_0^T v(t)\,dt$ — area under velocity-time curve
- **Work done by motor** = $\int_0^T \tau(t)\,\omega(t)\,dt$
- **Total heat** in calorimetry = $\int_0^T \dot{Q}(t)\,dt$
- **Mass** of non-uniform rod = $\int_0^L \rho(x)\,A(x)\,dx$

**Key references:** Apostol (1967, *Calculus*, 2nd ed.); Courant & John (1965, *Introduction to Calculus and Analysis*).
